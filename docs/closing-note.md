# Closing note

I enjoyed this task because it forced product decisions alongside the browser work. The interesting question for me was how much an agent could do while leaving the traveller with a clear choice and an honest account of the outcome. Once the basic booking path worked, I was most drawn to the chat experience: in a group conversation, progress, pauses and earlier decisions need to remain legible while the agent works.

I left out autonomous payment. I considered a stored card, Halcy paying and invoicing the traveller, and prepaid Halcy credit, but could not establish a sufficiently clear legal and technical route for them within this exercise. I kept the traveller in control of payment and final submission. I would revisit autonomous payment after a review of the real money, card data and browser paths.

I am least sure about how to confirm a booking across unfamiliar sites. A receipt in the browser is immediate and keeps the check in the same session, but its wording and layout may be hard to verify safely. A confirmation email could be an independent signal, provided the traveller chose to grant inbox access, but it may arrive late or disagree with the browser. I would test whether matching the hotel reference and stay details across both sources improves reliability before treating email as confirmation. Embedded payment pages and unclear preparatory buttons are other boundaries I would investigate further.

The prototype has completed a confirmed mock booking, and focused checks exercise repricing, hold-expiry handling, stale actions and the private handoff. Unfamiliar-site reliability remains unmeasured. Next I would test held-out local hotel layouts and measure verified completion, quote errors, unnecessary handoffs, time and model spend across successes and failures. I would also build a more native mobile journey: bottom sheets for offer and quote review, dialogs for important confirmations or interruptions, push notifications when the traveller is needed or a task finishes or fails, and an unmistakable privacy state—perhaps the agent covering its eyes—while the traveller controls checkout. With a larger baseline, I would compare smaller models stage by stage and add routing where it improves cost or completion.
